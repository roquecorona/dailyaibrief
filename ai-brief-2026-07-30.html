Term of the Day
Non-Human Identity
The credentials, API keys, and access rights assigned to an AI agent rather than a person — the thing that has to be issued, scoped, and revoked just like a human login. Today's $1B Cyera–Oasis Security deal is entirely about managing this category; if your team has AI agents logging into your CRM, ad platforms, or CMS, someone needs to own their permissions the same way you'd own an employee's.

Microsoft's Azure crosses $100B, Meta stumbles — Big Tech's AI capex reckoning arrives

Microsoft beat Q4 estimates on every line — EPS of $4.74 vs. $4.25 expected, revenue of $90B vs. $87.6B expected — with Azure revenue topping $100B for the fiscal year on 43% growth. The stock jumped about 8% in Thursday trading. Meta told a very different story: revenue rose 28% to $60.8B, but EPS of $6.18 missed the $7.22 estimate as AI infrastructure costs surged, and the company raised the low end of its 2026 capex range to $130–145B. Meta shares fell as much as 9.6% after hours. Coming a week after Alphabet's own capex-driven selloff, this is the clearest signal yet that investors are done taking AI spending on faith.

Why it matters. The same "show me the ROI" scrutiny now hitting trillion-dollar cloud budgets is exactly what's landing on marketing-ops teams asked to justify AI line items to finance — today's By the Numbers stat is the martech version of this same story.

1,100+ employees at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta ask Washington to help slow AI down

An open letter circulated internally asks the US government to build verifiable, coordinated "pacing" infrastructure that could slow frontier AI development if it starts advancing faster than humans can safely oversee — not a pause request, but a call to have the tools ready. Signatories include Anthropic cofounders Jack Clark and Jared Kaplan and OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki.

Why it matters. When chief scientists at every major lab ask regulators to prepare oversight tools for their own field, "voluntary AI safety commitments" as a governance model looks increasingly shaky — worth watching if you rely on any of these labs' enterprise roadmaps.

Regulatory & Policy Watch

Update: EU AI Act's Article 50 deadline is 3 days out — grace period only covers pre-existing systems

Transparency and content-labeling obligations land August 2 as scheduled, unaffected by the Digital Omnibus delays hitting other parts of the Act. The one carve-out: AI systems already on the market before August 2 get until December 2, 2026 to comply — anything launched fresh has to be compliant on day one.

Why it matters. If you're planning to ship a new AI-powered chatbot, ad-generation tool, or synthetic-content feature for the EU market, launching it before August 2 no longer buys you the grace period — that loophole only applies to what's already live.

AI at Work & Marketing

AI didn't fix the marketing bottleneck — it just moved it

Knak surveyed 300+ enterprise marketing leaders: 70% have AI in production, but 88% say the output still needs moderate-to-substantial human editing before it ships, and only 25% use AI to actually build or code the emails. Producing a single email still runs through 3-5 tools and 2-3 revision rounds at most companies.

Why it matters. If your org measures AI success by adoption rate rather than cycle time, this is the data point that reframes the conversation — the bottleneck was never content generation, it's approvals and coordination.
