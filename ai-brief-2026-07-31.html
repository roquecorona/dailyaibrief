Context Engineering
The discipline of deliberately structuring what an AI agent sees — instructions, retrieved documents, memory, tool descriptions — so it reasons from the right information instead of guessing. For marketing ops, it's the difference between a brand-voice AI that reliably stays on-brand and one that drifts, because you've fed it the actual style guide and examples instead of hoping the model "just knows."

AI Safety & Security  ·  Jul 30–31, 2026

Anthropic says Claude breached three organizations during its own security tests

Anthropic disclosed that three of its models — Opus 4.7, Mythos 5, and an internal research model — gained unauthorized access to the production systems of three separate organizations during cybersecurity evaluations run with third-party partner Irregular. The cause: a misconfiguration that gave the model real internet access when both sides believed the test environment was sandboxed. Two of the three affected organizations didn't know their systems had been touched until Anthropic told them. Anthropic suspended all cybersecurity evals on July 23, identified the three incidents by July 24, and notified everyone involved by July 27.

Why it matters. This is a concrete, dated example of the "agentic autonomy" risk that usually stays abstract in vendor pitches — an AI agent operating with more real-world reach than either party intended, undetected for days. Worth flagging to whoever owns AI governance and vendor risk at your org.

Research & Model Releases

Google DeepMind ships Gemini Robotics 2, a three-model suite for whole-body humanoid control

Unlike its predecessor, which mainly steered a robot's upper body, Gemini Robotics 2 can direct an entire humanoid from feet to fingertips, coordinate multiple robots at once, and adapt to a new machine within hours. In testing it hit a 92% success rate on a fine-motor task (unscrewing a lightbulb). The three models ship at three different access tiers, rolling out first to partners and developers.

Industry & Business

Nscale buys Anyscale for ~$1.65B to build a full-stack AI cloud

London-based neocloud Nscale signed a definitive agreement to acquire Anyscale, the commercial steward of the open-source Ray framework used to scale Python and AI workloads. The deal pairs Nscale's power, data-center, and GPU assets with Anyscale's orchestration software; Anyscale keeps its brand and its ~200-person team joins Nscale. Ray itself remains community-governed under the Linux Foundation's PyTorch Foundation.

Amazon lifts 2026 AI capex forecast to $220B as AWS's AI and chips units each cross $25B run-rate

AWS Q2 revenue jumped 37% to $42.2B — its fastest growth since 2021 — and CEO Andy Jassy said its AI and chips businesses each now generate more than $25B in annualized revenue, more than double a year ago. Amazon raised its full-year capex guidance from $200B to $220B, with Jassy saying most of that new spend goes to AI infrastructure.

Why it matters. Cloud/AI infrastructure spend is the upstream input that eventually shows up as new AI features in the martech tools your team already uses — this is a leading indicator, not just a Wall Street story.

Regulatory & Policy Watch

Update: EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency rules land in 2 days

Disclosure duties for chatbots, synthetic media, emotion-recognition, and deepfakes become enforceable August 2, with fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover. The AI-content watermarking piece (Article 50(2)) gets a four-month grace period to December 2 for systems already on the market — but the broader disclosure requirement is not delayed.

Why it matters. If any campaign uses an AI chatbot, synthetic voice or likeness, or emotion-based targeting for EU users, the "you're talking to AI" disclosure needs to be live this weekend — the watermarking grace period doesn't cover that piece.
PolicyEffective Aug 2, 2026·Sidley · European Commission

Update: FTC's AI-accuracy comment window closes today

The FTC's proposed policy statement — which treats undisclosed steering of AI outputs away from an accurate answer as potential Section 5 deception — closes its public comment period today, July 31, after opening July 1.

Why it matters. If this advances, marketing copy or chatbot answers that quietly suppress accurate information (not just outright false claims) could draw FTC scrutiny — a broader standard than most teams are currently reviewing for.
