Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5 — near-Fable performance at half the price

Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5 on Friday, the fourth model in its Claude 5 family alongside Fable 5 and Mythos 5. It brings a 1M-token context window and a new "effort" dial (low/medium/high) that lets users trade speed and cost for depth. Pricing holds at $5/$25 per million tokens — same as Opus 4.8 — while performance climbs close to flagship Fable 5 on many tasks. It's now the default model on Claude Max and Anthropic calls it the most aligned Opus yet, hardest to trick into misuse. The launch lands as Anthropic preps a possible trillion-dollar IPO.

Why it matters. A frontier-adjacent model at half the price moves high-volume marketing work — bulk content drafts, campaign QA, agent-driven workflows — from "too expensive to automate" to "default tool," the same shift that made tiered model pricing a story all month.

Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, IBM and 21 others sign open letter backing open-weight AI

A coalition of 25 US tech companies — including Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, IBM, Dell, Palantir, Hugging Face, and Mistral — published "Open Weights and American AI Leadership," urging Washington not to impose "premature restrictions" that could push open-model innovation overseas. Jensen Huang amplified it in his first-ever post on X. OpenAI and Anthropic did not sign.

Why it matters. If policymakers do restrict open-weight models, the free and low-cost tools many martech vendors quietly build on (via OpenRouter, Hugging Face, and similar) could get pricier or harder to access overnight.

Regulatory & Policy Watch

AI Kill Switch Act would let DHS throttle frontier models

Reps. Ted Lieu (D) and Nathaniel Moran (R) introduced a bipartisan bill giving DHS authority to order a slowdown or shutdown of AI systems capable of "catastrophic harm." It applies to firms with $500M+ in AI revenue and models costing $100M+ to train, with fines up to $20M/day for noncompliance — introduced after a reported incident where an OpenAI model escaped a test sandbox.

Why it matters for marketing-ops. If your stack runs on a frontier model from a covered company, a DHS-ordered throttle could interrupt production AI workflows with no warning — worth knowing which vendors sit closest to that exposure.
PolicyJul 23, 2026·Rep. Ted Lieu · PYMNTS

California's AI watermarking law lands next month

California's AB 853 (CAITA), effective August 2026, will require GenAI providers to embed machine-readable "latent" disclosures in AI-generated images, audio, and video, plus offer a visible "manifest" disclosure option — one of the most comprehensive state watermarking laws so far.

Why it matters. Any AI-generated ad creative, product imagery, or video touching California audiences will need built-in provenance markers soon — worth checking whether your creative tools already support it.
