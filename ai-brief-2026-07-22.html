Anthropic's $1.5B AI copyright settlement gets final court approval

A federal judge in San Francisco granted final approval Monday to Anthropic's record $1.5 billion settlement with authors and publishers who accused the company of training Claude on pirated books. The deal covers roughly 500,000 works at $3,000 apiece, with more than 91% of eligible rightsholders filing claims and $101M+ going to attorneys' fees. The underlying finding still stands: training on lawfully acquired books can be fair use, but stockpiling 7 million pirated copies in a "central library" is not.

Why it matters. This is the clearest legal line yet between "AI training as fair use" and "AI training on stolen data" — and it's the largest copyright settlement on record. Every AI-powered writing, image, or content tool a marketing team evaluates now sits closer to a real, priced legal standard for how its training data was sourced.

Regulatory & Policy Watch

EU's AI Act transparency rules still land Aug 2 — even as the heavier stuff slides to 2027

The Digital Omnibus on AI, signed July 8, pushed high-risk AI system obligations out to December 2027 (standalone systems) and August 2028 (embedded systems). But Article 50's transparency duties — disclosing AI chatbots, marking AI-generated content, labeling deepfakes — were not delayed and take effect on schedule August 2, 2026.

Why it matters. Eleven days out: if your team ships AI-generated ad creative, chatbot experiences, or synthetic content in the EU, this is the deadline to audit disclosure and labeling now, not the delayed high-risk rules everyone's talking about.
PolicySigned Jul 8; effective Aug 2, 2026·Technology.org · Gibson Dunn

France becomes first EU country to pass a social media ban for under-15s

France's parliament passed the ban 279–81 on Tuesday. New accounts for under-15s are blocked starting September 1; the rule extends to all existing accounts by January 2027. Platforms — TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, Snapchat, YouTube — bear the burden of age verification.

Why it matters. Age-gating at the platform level reshapes reach and targeting for any youth-adjacent campaign running in France starting this fall, and it's the first EU-wide test case for a fight likely to spread to other member states.

AI at Work & Marketing

NYT investigation: AI-generated "wellness influencers" are flooding social ads with misleading supplement claims

The New York Times found hundreds of hyper-realistic AI avatars posing as doctors and healers on Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok, pushing supplements with fabricated before-and-after transformations — often at older, health-vulnerable audiences. Reporting says Chinese ad shops are producing up to 1,200 such AI videos a day for U.S. TikTok Shop listings, and at least one product tied to the ads triggered an FDA/CDC Salmonella recall. No federal U.S. law currently restricts synthetic performers in advertising.

Why it matters. This is the cautionary case for anyone using AI avatars or UGC-style AI creative: enforcement is visibly behind the technology. Synthetic-performer disclosure laws like New York's (covered here previously) are the direction of travel — get ahead of labeling before a regulator or platform forces the issue.
