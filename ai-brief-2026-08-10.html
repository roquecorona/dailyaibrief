TSMC's July sales jump 45% as AI chip demand keeps defying market jitters

The world's largest contract chipmaker reported July revenue of NT$467.58 billion ($14.5B), up 44.7% year-over-year and a new monthly record, with high-performance computing — where TSMC books AI chip sales for customers like Nvidia and Google — now accounting for two-thirds of revenue. TSMC reiterated it expects 2026 revenue growth "slightly above 40%" and raised its capex outlook to $60–64B.

Why it matters. This lands the same week Meta's stock is nursing a historic losing streak over its AI spending, and it's the plainest real-world signal available on whether the AI capex cycle is actually funded by demand or just momentum — worth knowing before you sit through the next "is this a bubble" conversation at work.

Research Papers

SteerWrite: training-free steering for personalized co-writing

Tsinghua University researchers propose a training-free method that adapts a base LLM to a specific writer's voice or domain at the token level — no fine-tuning, no large datasets — by steering generation during co-writing rather than chat. In testing across multiple datasets and models, it cut the amount of human editing needed to reach a finished draft.

Why it matters. Most "brand voice" AI tools today lean on prompt engineering or costly fine-tuning. A cheap, swappable way to keep long-form drafts on-brand — without retraining a model every time guidelines change — is directly useful for content and email teams.

Regulatory & Policy Watch

20+ state AGs and civil-liberties groups warn FTC's AI-accuracy plan could backfire

Attorneys general from California, Colorado, and roughly 20 other states, along with EFF and the Center for Democracy and Technology, filed comments this month warning that the FTC's proposed policy on "suppression of accuracy" in AI systems could expose routine safety tuning and content moderation to federal deception liability — and put companies in a bind between state and federal AI rules.

Why it matters. If your team makes any claims about AI-generated content being "accurate" or moderates model outputs for brand safety, this is the fight that will decide how much legal exposure that creates — worth tracking even though it's still just a proposal.
PolicyFiled Aug 3, 2026·Tech Times · Bloomberg Law
