Model Routing
Automatically sending different AI tasks to different models based on cost and complexity — cheap, fast models for routine work, expensive frontier models only when the task actually needs one. OpenAI's three-tier Sol/Terra/Luna lineup (and this week's price cuts) is exactly this logic, and it's the same principle behind agent-orchestration tools inside modern martech stacks that route routine content work to cheap models and escalate only when needed.

OpenAI slashes GPT-5.6 API pricing by up to 80%

OpenAI cut prices on two of its three GPT-5.6 models this week: Luna, its cheapest tier, dropped 80% to $0.20 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens, while Terra fell 20% to $2/$12. Sol's price held steady, but OpenAI layered a paid "Fast mode" on top of it. Sam Altman confirmed the cuts publicly, calling them "major price cuts" driven by a more cost-sensitive customer base and mounting pressure from cheaper Chinese models like DeepSeek's V4 and Moonshot's Kimi K3.

Why it matters. This is the clearest sign yet that "good enough, cheap" is becoming the default tier for high-volume AI work — exactly the repetitive drafting, classification, and QA tasks that make up most marketing-ops AI usage. Worth revisiting your own AI tool budgets against this kind of downward pricing pressure, not just chasing new capabilities.

Research & Model Releases

DeepSeek moves V4-Flash out of preview, more than 7x's its coding score in one retrain

DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731, the production version of its Flash model — same 284B-parameter (13B active) architecture as the April preview, but retrained entirely around agentic and coding tasks. Terminal-Bench 2.1 jumped from 61.8 to 82.7, and its DeepSWE coding score rose from 7.3 to 54.4. It's priced at $0.14 per million input tokens and $0.27 per million output tokens — among the cheapest capable models on the market.

Industry & Business

Amazon quietly winds down most of its Nova AI models

Amazon is moving Nova Premier, Omni, Reel, and Canvas into "keep the lights on" mode and consolidating its AI effort into a new Frontier Model Research team led by Pieter Abbeel (from its 2024 Covariant acquisition). The reset follows a strategy — under former AI chief Rohit Prasad — that spread engineering resources across many model families without producing one that was truly frontier-competitive. A new flagship model, possibly still carrying the Nova name, is expected at re:Invent this fall.

Why it matters. One of the three biggest cloud vendors just admitted its in-house model strategy wasn't working. If your martech stack runs on Amazon Bedrock/Nova today, long-term investment there is explicitly not the priority right now.

Microsoft's Q4 beat triggers the largest single-day stock jump in its history

Microsoft shares surged 15.5% on July 30 — the biggest one-day gain in the company's history — after fiscal Q4 revenue hit $90B (+18% YoY) and Azure cloud revenue crossed $100B in annual revenue for the first time, with growth accelerating to 43%. After a rough year of AI-capex skepticism, Wells Fargo called it "a meaningful step in reversing the stock's multi-quarter period of underperformance."

Regulatory & Policy Watch

Update: the EU AI Act and California's CAITA both take effect tomorrow

Article 50 of the EU AI Act — disclosure duties for chatbots, synthetic media, and emotion-recognition systems — becomes enforceable August 2, with fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover (the AI-watermarking piece gets a grace period to December 2). California's AI Transparency Act (CAITA/AB 853), delayed twice already, lands the same day, requiring covered generative-AI platforms to offer detection tools and disclosure options for AI-generated image, video, and audio content.

Why it matters. If any campaign uses an AI chatbot, synthetic voice or likeness, or AI-generated image/video for EU or California audiences, the "you're talking to/looking at AI" disclosure needs to be live this weekend.
PolicyEffective Aug 2, 2026·European Commission · Natural Law Review

NSA and CISA's classified frontier-model review framework activates today

Under Executive Order 14409 (signed June 2), today is the 60-day deadline for the NSA, CISA, and other agencies to stand up a classified benchmarking process that designates "covered frontier models" with advanced cyber capabilities, plus a voluntary framework giving the federal government up to 30 days of pre-release access to them. The designation criteria stay classified — developers won't see the thresholds used.

Why it matters. This is direct federal government access to the same frontier models (GPT, Claude, Gemini) your martech vendors build on. Sam Altman confirmed this week that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are all working under the framework.
PolicyEffective Aug 1, 2026·Congress.gov CRS · The AI Daily Brief
